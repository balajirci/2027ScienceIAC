1. Tiny hairlike structures called cilia line parts of this body system and sweep mucus and trapped dust. Inside it, about 300 million tiny air sacs called alveoli [[al-VEE-oh-lye]] let oxygen pass into the blood while carbon dioxide passes out. The trachea, or windpipe, and a dome-shaped muscle called the diaphragm are also part of it. For the point, name this network of organs and tissues, including the lungs, that allows you to breathe.

Show answer

ANSWER: respiratory system [accept pulmonary system; prompt on lungs]

2. The second stage of this process, the Calvin cycle, takes place in the stroma, while its light-dependent reactions occur in stacks of thylakoids [[THY-lah-koyds]]. This process uses carbon dioxide and water to make glucose and releases oxygen. For the point, name this process in which plants use sunlight captured by chlorophyll to make their own food.

Show answer

ANSWER: photosynthesis

3. This scientist's work was published in 1866 but was ignored until it was rediscovered in 1900 by Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns, and Erich von Tschermak [vawn CHER-mak] . This Augustinian friar grew nearly 30,000 plants in the garden of St. Thomas's Abbey in Brno, studying traits such as seed shape and flower color. His laws of segregation and law of independent assortment describe how traits are passed on. For the point, name this "father of genetics" who experimented with pea plants.

Show answer

ANSWER: Gregor Mendel [or Gregor Johann Mendel]

4. A natural pacemaker in this organ called the sinoatrial node sends out electrical signals, and the "lub-dub" sound it makes comes from valves snapping shut. For the point, name this muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

Show answer

ANSWER: heart

5. Rosalind Franklin's X-ray image called "Photo 51" helped reveal the shape of this molecule. Its four bases are adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine, and adenine always pairs with thymine. In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick described this molecule as a "double helix," like a twisted ladder. For the point, name this molecule that carries genetic instructions in living things, which is shaped like a twisted ladder and is found in the nucleus of your cells.

Show answer

ANSWER: DNA [or deoxyribonucleic acid]

6. According to the endosymbiotic theory, this structure was once a free-living bacterium, which explains why it has its own small loop of DNA. 7. The mimic species of this animal can change its shape and color to look like a lionfish or a sea snake. For the point, name this eight-armed sea creature.

Show answer

ANSWER: octopus [or octopuses; accept octopodes or octopi]

8. This body has a 3:2 spin-orbit resonance, rotating three times on its axis for every two trips around the Sun, so a single solar day on it lasts about 176 Earth days. ANSWER: Mercury

ANSWER: Andromeda Galaxy [or M31; or Messier 31]
